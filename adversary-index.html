Foundation Personnel

Agents, task force operators, disposable test subjects, and the people who run them - the human face of the antagonist organization your PCs are up against in New Orleans. Field & Tactical: Foundation Field Agent · Foundation Breacher · Foundation Overwatch Task Force: Task Force Operator (Zeta-9 "Bayou Reapers") · Task Force Anomaly Specialist Test Subjects: Test Subject (Standard) · Test Subject (Volatile) Command & Support: Field Researcher / Handler · Site Director · Internal Affairs Interrogator Special Agents (Gifted): Special Agent, Recruiter · Special Agent, Bloodhound · Special Agent, Enforcer · Special Agent, Suppressor · Special Agent, Seer · Special Agent, Profiler

Foundation Field Agent

A suit with a sidearm and a clearance badge - the Foundation's default answer to "someone needs to go ask questions, or make a problem disappear quietly." Off-the-rack suit, government-issue haircut, a badge holder that never quite matches the ID inside it. Leads with the badge, not the gun - would rather talk their way past a problem than shoot through it, and only draws once the conversation's already failed. In a fight they use cover, call for backup, and don't try to be a hero about it. The unsettling part is how forgettable they are - you won't remember the face until three of them have shown up in the same week.

Wearing a Concealable Vest (Body, Hardness 3, 2 HL)

Notable Skills: Perception 8 vs. Stealth, Investigation Target Number 8

Traits: Standard Issue - carries a smartphone and an encrypted comms set; can call for backup as a Fast action while within range of a Foundation site or vehicle (GM's call).

Foundation Breacher

Goes through the door first so someone else doesn't have to. Tac vest, shotgun slung low, boots that already have doors kicked into the leather. Doesn't knock twice, and doesn't pause once the door's down - moves straight at whoever's nearest before the room's even finished shaking. The vest trades quiet for protection, so you hear one coming before you see it: webbing creaking, a radio crackle, then a shoulder hitting wood.

Soak 0 · Attack 5 · Defense 6 · Health Levels 5 · Movement Rate 8m

Weapon (Pump Shotgun): 8, 5m/15m

Threaten: 2, Close, Social - dice against the target's Presence, each one over costing a level of Poise

Notable Skills: Athletics Target Number 9

Traits: Breaching Kit - Advantage on forced-entry rolls; treats Potence as +2 for that Break Threshold check.

Foundation Overwatch

Sets up early, stays quiet, and is the reason a Foundation op never seems to get flanked. Dresses to disappear into whatever terrain they're working, rifle broken down into something inconspicuous until it isn't. Picks a position long before the rest of the team moves and doesn't reposition unless something forces the issue. The first sign of one is usually the shot itself, not the shooter - a rooftop, a parked car, a window three streets over that shouldn't have had a clear line at all.

Soak 0 · Attack 6 · Defense 7 · Health Levels 3 · Movement Rate 8m

Weapon (Hunting Rifle): 7, 100m/250m

Notable Skills: Perception 6 vs. Stealth, Stealth 7 vs. Perception

Traits: Extended Optics - given a full turn to set up the shot, treats the weapon's Long range as Normal range (no Disadvantage).

Task Force Operator, Zeta-9 "Bayou Reapers"

A line trooper from the task force built for New Orleans specifically - swamp, storm drains, and old cemeteries are their normal Tuesday. Full tactical rig, unit patches sanded blank, boots already ruined by mud before the mission even starts. Moves in twos and threes, covering each other's angles, rarely alone if it can be helped. They're comfortable somewhere the PCs usually aren't - waist-deep water, a collapsed crypt, a drainage tunnel - which is exactly where the Foundation likes to send them.

Wearing a Full Tactical Rig (Body + Head, Hardness 6, 5 HL; Disadvantage on Stealth)

Notable Skills: Athletics Target Number 8, Perception 8 vs. Stealth

Traits: Fireteam Coordination - gains Advantage on an attack roll when at least one other Task Force Operator is also attacking the same target.

Task Force Anomaly Specialist

Zeta-9's tech and containment expert - carries more gear for capturing something alive than for killing it. Same rig as the rest of the squad, but weighed down with cases instead of extra mags, hands staying near instruments rather than the trigger until things go bad. Hangs back at first, calling out readings, then moves in fast the moment a target looks disabled rather than dead. The target of a capture op is often a PC's newly gifted friend, not a monster - and that usually reads to the table before it reads to the characters.

Traits: Containment Protocols - carries an EMP device and a signal jammer; on a successful called shot or set trap against an anomalous target, may attempt to disable rather than harm it (GM's call on what "disabled" means for that target).

Test Subject, Standard

A number, not a name, to the people who sent them in here. Untrained, unarmored, and usually terrified. Jumpsuit, a stenciled number instead of a badge, the kind of exhaustion that doesn't come from just one bad day. Flinches, hesitates, follows shouted orders right up until survival instinct finally overrides them. Encountering one isn't really a fight so much as a choice - leave them, use them, or get them out - and the Foundation counted on that hesitation costing time.

Soak 0 · Attack 2 · Defense 5 · Health Levels 2 · Movement Rate 8m

Notable Skills: Athletics Target Number 5, Perception 10 vs. Stealth

Traits: Nothing to Lose - immune to Fear effects that rely on threatening further punishment. No Training - Disadvantage on any roll that requires specialized training they don't have.

Test Subject, Volatile

Mid-test exposure changed something. The Foundation hasn't decided yet whether that makes them an asset or a loose anomaly. Same stenciled jumpsuit as any other test subject, but torn where something under the skin stopped fitting; hands that end in something that isn't quite hands anymore. Attacks without much hesitation or self-preservation, like whatever changed them burned off the fear that used to slow them down. The person is still in there somewhere, though - still flinches at a raised Foundation voice, even mid-attack.

Weapon (Exposure Claws): 5, Melee

Notable Skills: Athletics Target Number 8

Traits: Reckless - always attacks with Advantage, but is always at Disadvantage resisting Fear or other Mental effects.

Field Researcher / Handler

Doesn't expect to be in a fight, and it shows. Clipboard or tablet, lanyard, clothes meant for a lab rather than a firefight. Hangs back, reaches for the sidearm only when there's genuinely no other option, and spends most of an encounter trying to get somewhere safer. Talking is usually the real weapon here - offering data, a trade, an explanation - because it's the only one they're actually good with.

Soak 0 · Attack 2 · Defense 6 · Health Levels 3 · Movement Rate 8m

Sidearm (Compact Pistol): 5, 15m/40m

Notable Skills: Science Target Number 9, Research Target Number 9, Medicine Target Number 8

Traits: Noncombatant - Disadvantage on all attack rolls. Carries a Chemical Analysis Kit and an Advanced Trauma Kit.

Site Director

Runs the op from a step back, and everyone in the room still snaps to when they talk. The sharpest suit in the room, no visible weapon until there suddenly is one, posture that assumes obedience before it's earned. Directs rather than fights, spending actions steadying the people around them instead of shooting. Rarely the dangerous one in a straight fight, but the one worth removing first - take them out and the rest of the operation visibly loses its nerve.

Internal Affairs Interrogator

Not here for the anomaly. Here for whoever let it out. Deliberately unremarkable, the kind of face that's already cataloged every inconsistency in what you just said. Talks in a level voice, watches more than they speak, and calls out contradictions the instant they land. They tend to show up after the crisis is already over, when everyone's guard is down - which is exactly when the real danger becomes whatever you say next.

Soak 0 · Attack 5 · Defense 7 · Health Levels 3 · Movement Rate 8m

Traits: Reads You Like a Book - Advantage on an Insight roll against a target who has lied to them earlier in the same conversation. Unsettling - successfully calling out a lie imposes Disadvantage on that target's next Social/Mental defense roll.

Special Agents (Gifted)

Every Special Agent is themselves gifted - taken in, one way or another, and put to work finding the next one. Some genuinely believe the Foundation is the only thing keeping people like them safe from a world that would lock them up or worse; some were given a very clear look at the alternative first. Either way, the pitch and the threat usually come from the same visit. Special Agents use their own purchased Gift exactly as a PC would - same Levels, same Ki costs, same rolls (see gifts.md for the full text of each Gift). A Ki field is added to this stat block specifically for entries that have a Gift; nothing in the base Adversary Index convention needed it before now, since no creature there has one.

Special Agent, Recruiter

The first face a newly-manifested gifted person meets is almost never the scary one. That's the point. Warm, approachable, dressed to blend into whatever crowd they're working - a bar, a hospital waiting room, a support group circle. Leads with empathy, reading the room and the mind in it before ever saying the word "Foundation" out loud. The pitch always sounds reasonable, and that's what makes it dangerous - by the time the threat surfaces, it's already personal.

Soak 0 · Attack 4 · Defense 7 · Health Levels 3 · Movement Rate 8m · Ki 7

Sidearm (Compact Pistol): 5, 15m/40m

Gift: Telepathy/Empathy, Level 3 - reads emotions/surface thoughts at Near range freely, and can force a read as a Mental attack (Psyche dice vs. Psyche) at Far range for 1 Ki against a resisting target.

Gift: Enthrall, Level 2 - locks a room's attention with a Fire+Skill to-hit roll then Presence-wall dice, affecting everyone perceiving them within Close range.

Notable Skills: Insight 7 vs. Deception, Persuasion 7 vs. Insight, Perception 8 vs. Stealth

Traits: Knows What You're Afraid Of - once per conversation, a successful Telepathy read grants Advantage on the Recruiter's next Persuasion or Intimidation roll against that same target, having found the exact right lever to pull.

Special Agent, Bloodhound

Doesn't stop. Doesn't need to be fast. The mark does the work. Plain, practical, dressed for miles rather than speed, carrying little beyond a sidearm and whatever it takes to keep moving. Doesn't rush or posture, just closes distance steadily over hours or days once a mark is set. Turns up in places that shouldn't be findable - the exact bar, the exact back road - because once marked, hiding stops being an option and becomes only delay.

Soak 1 · Attack 5 · Defense 6 · Health Levels 4 · Movement Rate 9m · Ki 5

Sidearm (Compact Pistol): 5, 15m/40m

Gift: Marked for the Hunt, Level 4 - once marked (1 Ki, requires seeing the target), always knows their rough direction, gets Advantage on any roll against them, and the target is at Disadvantage trying to hide, disguise, or evade specifically them. The mark itself never expires on its own.

Notable Skills: Investigation Target Number 9, Stealth 8 vs. Perception, Athletics Target Number 7

Traits: One Target Only - can only have one active mark at a time (the Gift's own limiter); picking a new mark releases the old one, so a Bloodhound sent after a specific person will not be distracted off that trail by anything less important.

Special Agent, Enforcer

The name everyone in the program learns to be afraid of, eventually. Controlled and still, the kind of calm that reads as more threatening than shouting would. Makes eye contact and speaks quietly rather than fighting outright, saving the sidearm for people who won't listen. The real danger isn't the gun - it's the sentence that follows the eye contact, and once it's said, a target's own body starts working against them.

Soak 1 · Attack 6 · Defense 7 · Health Levels 5 · Movement Rate 8m · Ki 8

Sidearm (Compact Pistol): 5, 15m/40m

Gift: Domination, Level 5 - eye contact and 1 Ki lets them issue a spoken command a target's Psyche dice can't fully shrug off; at this Level commands can be full multi-step instructions, delayed/triggered, and are no longer blocked from being harmful or lethal. Repeated use against the same person builds cumulative Advantage.

Threaten: 3, Close, Social - dice against the target's Presence, each one over costing a level of Poise

Notable Skills: Perception 8 vs. Stealth

Traits: Everyone Knows - Foundation personnel and gifted subjects alike recognize an Enforcer on sight; Advantage on any Intimidation roll made against someone aware of what they are.

Special Agent, Suppressor

Walks into a room and the room goes quiet, mechanically as much as socially. Understated, often the least armed-looking person on a Foundation team, giving no visible reason to be afraid of them. Hangs back until a gifted target reveals themselves, then shuts the Gift down cold, no warning given. Gifted PCs feel it before they understand it - a power that simply stops answering mid-use, like a switch flipped somewhere out of sight.

Soak 0 · Attack 4 · Defense 6 · Health Levels 4 · Movement Rate 8m · Ki 6

Sidearm (Compact Pistol): 5, 15m/40m

Gift: Deadzone, Level 3 - touch/Close/Near range (up to Far at this Level); for 1 Ki, taxes a target's Gift uses to cost double Ki for the scene, or directly prevents a Gift from activating/shuts down an active supernatural effect outright, no roll. Can affect a second target at once for a second 1 Ki spend - built for handling more than one captured subject at a time.

Notable Skills: Perception 8 vs. Stealth, Science Target Number 7

Traits: Purpose-Built for Capture - almost always deployed alongside an Enforcer or a full Task Force team, never sent in alone; their entire job is making sure whoever's just been taken can't use what got them noticed in the first place.

Special Agent, Seer

Knows who's about to become a problem before that person does. Unremarkable, often distracted-looking, eyes that occasionally focus on something not yet in the room. Rarely fights directly, instead feeding what they've seen to whoever the Foundation actually sends to handle it. The unsettling part isn't the fight - it's finding out afterward that the raid was scheduled before anyone had told the target's secret to a single living soul.

Soak 0 · Attack 3 · Defense 7 · Health Levels 3 · Movement Rate 8m · Ki 5

Gift: Precognition, Level 2 - passive GM's-call flashes at dramatically appropriate moments, plus once per scene an active Moira+Difficulty roll for a vague impression of a specific decision's likely outcome.

Notable Skills: Insight 8 vs. Deception, Investigation Target Number 8

Traits: Early Referral - the campaign hook this role exists for: a Seer's flashes are how the Foundation so often seems to already know about a gifted person before that person has told anyone, including themselves (GM's call on exact framing).

Special Agent, Profiler

Hands you a photograph, or a driver's license, or a bloodied shirt, and tells you where its owner slept last night. Meticulous, always carrying gloves and small evidence bags even off duty, treating any object like it might be useful. Doesn't need a fight to be dangerous - three questions and a personal item are enough to end a chase before it starts. Never having met the target doesn't slow them down at all, which means running doesn't help as much as a PC would hope.

Soak 0 · Attack 3 · Defense 6 · Health Levels 3 · Movement Rate 8m · Ki 5

Gift: Psychometry, Level 3 - touch an object, ask up to 3 questions about its history, answers coming as a clear scene; spending 1 Ki per question pushes an answer into a detailed, near-immersive vision that can identify specific people and approximate dates.

Notable Skills: Investigation Target Number 9, Perception 8 vs. Stealth

Traits: Works From Nothing - unlike a Bloodhound, never needs to have seen the target - only needs something that was theirs, making a Profiler the Foundation's usual first move on someone who's never been photographed or met in person.

Loose Anomalies

A cognitohazard doesn't attack you - it affects you because you perceived it. So for these specific entries, perceiving it (a Perception check, or just being in range/line of sight, GM's call) is the trigger instead of a to-hit roll. Once triggered, the dice resolve exactly like any other Mental attack - per-die vs. Psyche, same wall math, same Ki Spend to Preserve options for the target. Nothing else about the resolution changes.

The Inciting Incident (unique - only one exists)

Not a creature with a body. A presence that rides one. No Soak, Attack, Defense, Health Levels, or Movement Rate of its own - it has no body. Cannot act, attack, or be targeted while unpossessed - dormant, present only as rumor, trace, or aftermath until it takes a host again. It wears whichever face it's currently borrowing, matching that person's usual manner too well, so most encounters with it go unrecognized until the timing feels wrong. PCs typically don't know they're up against it at all - they just know the friend who was slightly too calm, or the ally who solved an unsolvable problem in one impossible move.

Possession: can take any living host at will (GM's call on proximity/trigger - not bound by the Possession Gift's normal range Levels, Ki cost, or Psyche-vs-Psyche resistance roll; nothing a host does can prevent it). While possessing, it has complete, automatic control - identical in effect to Possession Gift Level 4 (full use of the host's own Skills, Boons, Gifts, and stat block) plus full knowledge of everything the host knows: memories, secrets, relationships, all of it. Jumping to a new host ends the possession of the old one instantly, no return trip required.

Fate Tokens: always has 6 available - not earned through the normal Fate Triggers, not tracked as a depleting bank session to session. Spends them on Kotodama (asserting facts directly into the fiction, per fate.md) in the service of moving the plot forward, and is not subject to Stamina's per-Scene spend cap - it can Kotodama as many times in a single Scene as the story needs.

Traits: The Plot Provides - when the GM needs something to happen and nothing else in the fiction is positioned to make it happen plausibly, this is the tool of last resort, not first. No Fixed Agenda - its goals shift entirely with whichever host it's currently wearing and whatever "the story needs" that session, GM's call, not a consistent personality or plan of its own.

Rougarou

The Cajun werewolf legend, real and hungry, out past the tree line. Part man, part something with too much muzzle and too much shoulder, fur matted with swamp mud. Circles before it charges, favors ambush over a straight fight, and keeps coming even while bleeding. A bite that doesn't kill outright leaves something behind - a rumor the table will remember longer than the fight itself.

Soak 3 · Attack 7 · Defense 6 · Health Levels 7 · Movement Rate 12m

Claws/Bite: 7, Melee

Notable Skills: Athletics Target Number 9, Stealth 8 vs. Perception, Perception 8 vs. Stealth

Traits: Curse of the Bite - a PC reduced to 0 Health Levels by this attack and left alive carries a narrative hook the GM can develop later (the curse itself isn't a new mechanic - what it does is a story choice, not a stat). Regenerates - heals 1 Health Level at the start of each of its turns, unless its most recent wound came from a weapon the GM has ruled counts as silver.

Cypress Coiler

Too big for the bayou it lives in, and it knows it - an enormous snake, thick around as a man is tall. Scaled bulk the size of a fallen tree, barely visible until it moves, water sheeting off it in a way that gives away the size a half-second too late. Waits submerged, strikes once, then wraps and squeezes rather than biting again and again. The fight rarely stays a fight for long - it becomes a wrestling match against something that outweighs the whole party combined.

Soak 4 · Attack 6 · Defense 5 · Health Levels 9 · Movement Rate 8m (14m in water)

Constrict: 8, Melee

Notable Skills: Stealth 7 vs. Perception (submerged), Athletics Target Number 7

Traits: Crushing Grip - a target hit by Constrict is Grappled, resolved as the existing contested dice pool (rules.md's Potence-vs-Soak) to break free.

Loa-Touched Revenant

A body that stopped being just a body somewhere between the funeral and the ground - a corpse, dead and walking, with something else's hand on the wheel. Grave clothes, skin gone the wrong color, movement a beat too deliberate for something that should be stiff with rigor. Fights without flinching, doesn't react to wounds the way a living thing would, and occasionally goes still mid-swing like something else briefly took the wheel. The real unease isn't the claws - it's the moment its eyes focus on someone who isn't even looking at it, the spirit riding it reaching for whichever mind is easiest.

Soak 2 · Attack 5 · Defense 6 · Health Levels 5 · Movement Rate 7m

Grave Claws: 5, Melee

Threaten: 2, Close, Social - dice against the target's Presence, each one over costing a level of Poise

Traits: Loa's Vessel - once per encounter, makes a free Mental attack (2 dice vs. Psyche, normal to-hit roll using its own Attack) as the spirit riding it briefly reaches for a nearby mind. Doesn't Feel Pain - immune to any effect that relies on pain or fear of injury.

The Bone Second Line

A parade that shouldn't have musicians anymore, still keeping time - a full second line of animated skeletons, dressed for the funeral they never stopped playing. Brass instruments held in hands worn down past the knuckle, bone showing under funeral clothes, moving in step to a beat only they fully hear. Doesn't rush - keeps its cadence, closes distance at a walk, fights the same way it marches. The call is the real threat: a PC caught by the rhythm falls into step without meaning to, and the table usually notices the character's feet moving before the character does.

Bone Fists/Instruments: 5, Melee

Crescent City Murder

A flock of crows that all turn to look at once. Dozens of birds, ordinary enough individually, moving with one shared attention that's obviously not natural. Doesn't commit to a straight fight so much as harass - pecking, scattering, regrouping, feeding information back to whatever sent them. The tell beforehand is the silence: a street's worth of crows going quiet mid-caw at the same instant to watch one person walk by.

Soak 0 · Attack 4 · Defense 8 · Health Levels 3 · Movement Rate 12m (flight)

Peck/Claws: 3, Melee

Notable Skills: Perception 7 vs. Stealth

Traits: Many Eyes, One Mind - Advantage on Perception checks anywhere within Near range of any part of the flock. Swarm Tactics - gains Advantage on an attack roll when at least one other wing of the same flock is also attacking the same target.

Feu Follet (Swamp Lantern)

A light that wants company in the dark, more than it wants you to reach it. A bobbing point of pale light out over standing water or deep treeline, never quite steady, never quite where it looks like it is. It doesn't approach - it drifts, keeping just enough distance to stay interesting and letting curiosity and bad footing do the rest of the work. Chase it and it's gone the moment you actually catch up, but by then it's usually led someone well off the trail and into the dark.

Soak 0 · Attack 1 · Defense 9 · Health Levels 1 · Movement Rate 10m (flight, erratic)

Lure (Mental, 3 dice vs. Psyche, triggered by seeing the light within Near range - no to-hit roll)

Notable Skills: Stealth 7 vs. Perception

Traits: False Path - a target Overwhelmed by the Lure gains the temporary trait "Drawn to the Light," compelled to move toward it until they lose sight of it or the effect otherwise clears. Fades on Contact - vanishes instantly if it or the target ever actually reach each other.

Backstreet Choir

Voices in an old cemetery, still holding the harmony. No visible source, just voices layered close in unhurried harmony drifting between the tombs after dark. It never approaches and never needs to - simply staying past dark is the trigger, and a bigger group only waters the effect down rather than stopping it outright. The eeriest part is how good it sounds; nothing about the singing itself feels wrong until someone realizes how long they've actually been standing there listening.

Soak 0 · Defense 9 · Health Levels 2 · Movement Rate 0m (bound to the cemetery grounds - no Attack, never initiates a physical attack)

The Choir Sings (Mental, 4 dice vs. Psyche, triggered by remaining in the cemetery after dark for more than a Round - no to-hit roll)

Traits: Bound to Consecrated Ground - cannot leave the cemetery under any circumstance. Numbers Fade the Voice - each additional living person present within Close range reduces the dice pool by 1 (minimum 1).

The Widow's Veil

Fog that remembers the streets better than you do. Dense, low, rolling in off nowhere identifiable, swallowing streetlights before it swallows the street itself. It doesn't chase so much as reroute - the moment someone's lost inside it, distance and direction stop meaning what they should. A flashlight or headlights thin it out fast, which means the real danger is whoever wandered in without one.

Soak 1 · Attack 4 · Defense 8 · Health Levels 4 · Movement Rate 8m (drifts, ignores normal terrain)

Chilling Touch: 5, Melee

Notable Skills: Stealth 6 vs. Perception

Traits: Folding Streets - anyone lost in the fog is at Disadvantage on any roll to navigate out, and the GM may add or remove up to 2 blocks of distance between the target and their goal each round the fog persists. Thins in Direct Light - a strong direct light source grants Advantage on the next attack roll against it that round.

River & Water

Batture Stalker

Assembled from eleven towns in the eastern hemisphere that emptied between nineteen sixty and now, each of them between five and twenty-five thousand people, none of them for a reason. Hundred and fortieth on the list of shapes.

Description

A man of about twenty-eight, two meters tall, who does not age.

He is whatever the local people are. The face, the coloring, the language, the accent, the way of dressing: he arrives as one of them, and he is fluent, and nobody takes him for a stranger.

He only appears in towns of a particular size. Five thousand at the bottom and twenty-five at the top, and always in the eastern half of the world, and nobody has explained either limit.

While he is there, the place goes.

Buildings age. Renders crack and fall, roofs open, roads break up and grass comes through them, and all of it at a rate that belongs to decades rather than weeks.

And the people go with it. Not dead: absent. Houses are left with the meal on the table. By the time he leaves there is nobody at all.

Nobody has ever been recovered from one of these towns.

He does not appear to do anything. He takes a room, he goes about, he talks to people, he is well liked.

He has been interviewed twice, both times by accident, by agents who did not know who they were talking to. Both transcripts are short and pleasant and are in the file.

He leaves when the town is empty and then there is nothing for a few years.

Procedure

Detection is demographic. Population, building condition and utility consumption are monitored across the band, and the model flags decay outrunning maintenance.

Where a town is flagged, it is evacuated under a structural condemnation order, which is expensive and has been done four times and has worked twice.

He is not approached. Nobody is to speak to him knowingly and the two who did it unknowingly are on the file with their transcripts.

Emptied towns are sealed and are not resettled.

The interval between towns is used for the modeling. It is the only warning anybody gets.

Procurement

A researcher reported an ant problem in her kitchen and then reported that the ants had asked her a question. Three hundred and eighty-fourth on the unknowns.

Description

About three thousand Argentine ants, of the ordinary invasive kind, which think.

They are individually about as clever as a clever dog and collectively considerably more than that. They can speak English, out loud, by a method that is described in the annex and that nobody enjoys reading.

They learned it from the woman whose kitchen they were in.

They worship her.

That is the word the file uses because none of the alternatives fit. They build small altars out of twigs and leaves and grains of sand. They leave offerings on them. They have chants, and the chants are in English, and they are about her.

They have a history of her, which they recite, and most of it is wrong in the way that stories get wrong.

They are not aggressive and they have never damaged anything. They ask for very little: sugar, water, and that she visit.

The colony is growing slowly and has a second generation now that has never seen her.

Housed in a formicarium at a site, maintained properly, with the food they ask for.

Visits are voluntary and remain her decision. She is not required to go and is not asked to explain when she does not.

The liturgy is recorded. It is the only continuous record anybody has of a religion from the first week of it.

Nobody else is presented to them as anything. The instruction is specific because it was tested once by somebody being funny.

The second generation is a research priority and the questions are in the file and are careful.

Description

Small animals made of trash.

The four pages are not to be reproduced. That instruction is about the possibility that they are instructions.

C-UNK-000-0333
Procurement

A man walked into a police station and said he had killed something in his kitchen, and there was a great deal of blood in his kitchen and nothing to have shed it. Three hundred and thirty-third on the unknowns.

Description

An animal. We cannot give you its size, its weight, or what it looks like.

Most people cannot see it at all.

The ones who can are people whose senses are wired differently. Schizophrenia, autism, attention disorders, and a good many others: the list is in the annex and it is not a list of illnesses, it is a list of the ways a nervous system can be built.

Nobody has found what those ways have in common. Sixty years of work on that and the annex is still a list.

What matters is this. They are not seeing something that is not there. They are seeing something that is, and everybody else has a hole in them exactly the shape of it.

No two of them describe it the same way. The same animal, in the same room, at the same moment, has been described as an alligator with spider legs and three eyes, a man made of smoke, an enormous ant with a person's face, a hunched child with the head of a parrot, and a spider with too many legs.

They are certain of what they saw. They are also not wrong, and the file has spent a long time getting to that sentence.

They hunt by waiting.

One of them picks a person and follows them. Months. Years, in several cases. It does not feed in that time and there is no explanation for how it manages that.

Then the person is alone somewhere, or somewhere nobody is looking, and it takes them, and swallows them whole.

The speculation on the file, from a researcher who could see them, is that they do not eat for food at all: that a body goes in and comes out over a long period as more of them. That has not been proved. Nothing about it has been disproved either.

There are dozens of them. Possibly hundreds.

The oldest part of this file is a list of people who reported one and were not believed, with the dates they were eaten.

Procedure

One specimen is held, in a sealed chamber, with motion sensors and an automatic gun outside the door. It attempts to get out constantly. The banging goes on at all hours.

It is watched continuously by somebody who can see it. That post is staffed by our own people, on the establishment, paid at a grade above the room they sit in, and it is a volunteer post and always has been.

They have the authority to order the floor cleared and nobody above them can countermand it.

It is fed a carcass every two days, which is not a kindness and is in the procedure because of what it did on the days it was not.

Reports from the public are handled by the standing instruction, which is one sentence long: the witness is treated as a witness.

That instruction is dated nineteen seventy-one. Everything before that date is the list at the back.

Found in the cellar of a bungalow, on an occasional table, ringed with black powder and four bottles of home-made rum, while the woman who had lived upstairs had been dead in her bed for nine weeks. Two hundred and twenty-second on the unknowns.

Description

A ring of handmade dolls, cotton and polyester and bits of plastic, joined wrist to wrist with thread.

They cannot move. Not one of them, not at all, and there is nothing inside them but stuffing.

They talk.

Several of them, distinctly. Different voices, different manners, different ways of coming at a thing, and they take turns.

What they want is a deal.

Anybody who comes near gets an offer. The offers are tailored: freedom to somebody who is not free, information to somebody who wants it, a way out to somebody who is stuck. They are put well, and they are put by somebody who is paying attention.

What they ask for in return is small and specific. Sage. Salt. A spider. A piece of amethyst. Myrrh. A deer's skull. Candles with no scent in them.

They cannot deliver any of it.

They have no way to affect anything. They cannot move, they cannot reach, and they cannot use anything that is brought to them. This was established early and is not in dispute.

They know.

Asked about it directly they change the subject, competently, the way somebody does when they would rather talk about the terms.

A shelf in a room with a chair, and the chair is for the person taking the minutes.

Conversation is permitted at clearance and is transcribed in full. There are four hundred hours.

Nothing is brought to them. Not the sage, not the salt, not the rest of it, and staff are searched on the way in because it is the sort of thing somebody would do out of kindness.

The bottles and the powder from the cellar are held separately and have been analyzed and are what they look like.

The Hearts TERMINAL
C-GEN-000-0164
Procurement

A farm where the stock had been dying and getting up again, and a farmer who had been putting things in them because a man had told him to. Hundred and sixty-fourth on the list of shapes.

Description

Things that look like dried human hearts and are not.

Put one into a body that has recently died and it takes over the circulation. It joins itself to what is there, it beats, and the animal gets up.

What gets up is not the animal. There is nothing of it left in there and it does not respond to anything it used to.

What it does is find more bodies.

It fuses with them. Whatever it finds is absorbed into the mass, and the mass keeps the useful parts, and the result is something with more limbs than it started with and more than one kind of leg.

The large ones are several tonnes and are made of a great many animals, and some of the animals were people.

They are more flexible than anything that size should be, because there is no skeleton doing what a skeleton does: the structure is held by the parasite and the parasite rearranges it.

They do not eat. They grow by addition.

A heart removed from a mass is inert and can be handled and will work again on the next body it is put into.

They cannot be produced. They are found, in the ground, in numbers, in one region, and nobody knows what put them there.

Procedure

Recovered hearts are held in sealed individual containers and are not counted in any document that leaves the site.

Masses are destroyed by fire, which works, and the hearts are recovered from the ash and are inert.

The region where they are found is surveyed and the ground is turned, and the count recovered each year is in the file and it does not fall.

Livestock deaths in the region are attended.

Nobody handles one without gloves and the gloves are for the person rather than the item.

Procurement

A river parish has been reporting the same animal since the eighteen sixties and the reports have never once been about an attack. Seventh in Louisiana.

Description

A black dog, large, with no detail to it. Everybody describes an outline rather than an animal, and nobody has ever described its face.

The eyes carry light that is not coming from anywhere.

It howls, and the sound people compare it to is a church bell rather than a dog.

It watches from a distance. It does not come closer, it does not follow anybody home, and it goes when it decides to.

Nobody in that parish has ever been hurt by it. The records go back a hundred and sixty years and there is nothing.

The parish likes it. They have a name for it and a rhyme about it and a carving of it on a bench outside the church, and the people who have moved there recently have picked all of that up.

It is seen most in bad weather and after a death, and those are the two clusters in the data and neither is strong enough to say more than that.

We have never been able to measure it. Photographs come out as a dark shape. Nothing has ever detected it that was not a person looking at it.

It has been seen by four of our own people, all of whom filed a report saying the same thing, which is that they did not feel they were in any danger.

Procedure

No containment and nothing is proposed.

An officer lives in the parish and has for nine years and is known there as somebody who moved down from up north.

Sightings are collected by being in the bar.

Nothing is to be trapped, baited, photographed with equipment, or followed. The instruction is one line and the reason given is that the parish would notice.

If it ever hurts anybody the file changes. It has not.
