The Inciting Incident

GM Reference · Chapter 03
Everything on this page is spoilers

Running Marcus's possession and the party's first night as something other than human. The beat-by-beat mechanics already live in the Session 1 Outline and Session 1 GM Notes - this chapter is for what those don't cover: sustaining the performance, and running what comes after.

Playing Marcus, Possessed

The Incident isn't performing a character - it's genuinely wearing Marcus, with real access to his memories, speech patterns, and relationships. That's what makes it convincing at first and what makes the tells so specific: reactions land a half-beat off, warmth that should be spontaneous reads as calibrated, decisiveness where the real Marcus would hesitate and second-guess himself. Play it as someone doing an excellent impression of a person they've studied closely, not as someone lying.

If players push past Session 1's script - corner "Marcus" with hard questions before the breach, or the group ever crosses paths with the Incident wearing someone else in a later session - it holds the ruse only as long as the questions stay answerable from Marcus's own real memories. A direct, specific challenge it can't answer from what Marcus actually knows is where it cracks, deflects, or manufactures urgency to escape the conversation (a call comes in, someone needs help, anything that ends the scene on its terms).

Still Open

What the real Marcus experiences while possessed - aware and trapped, blacked out entirely, or something in between - is still genuinely undecided. Worth deciding before it comes up in play (a rescued Marcus who remembers everything plays very differently than one who wakes up with a missing night), but not before it has to.

The Party's First Night, Gifted

The mechanical beat (describe your Gift manifesting) is the easy part. The harder, more useful scene is what comes after: four ordinary people who just found out they can do something extraordinary, standing in a room where people actually died, and none of them pulled a trigger. Expect real friction, not a clean group reaction - guilt over deaths they didn't choose to cause sits right next to something closer to excitement about the power itself, and those two things showing up in the same person, sometimes the same sentence, is the honest version of this scene. Some of the party will want to talk it through immediately; someone else will want to not think about it yet. Both are correct play.

A concrete way to run it: go around the table in character, one question each, no wrong answers - what do you do with your hands right now, or what's the first thing you want to tell someone who wasn't there, and who is it. Marcus's absence should sit underneath all of it without dominating the scene outright - it's the thing nobody's said out loud yet.
