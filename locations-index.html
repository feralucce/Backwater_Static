Register Files

Thirty-two locations out of the Foundation's own register that belong in this city. These are files rather than dossiers: everything above GM Information is the Foundation talking, and whether any of it is correct is a separate question. Drawn from the Backwater Static supplement in the Foundation register.

Places the water made

The drainage, the ground under it, and what the ground does when it stops holding.

Our Lady of the Water ACTIVE
L-StB-840-0001 · Anomaly
Procurement

Came to the Foundation as a title deed in a bankrupt estate, along with nine acres of marsh nobody could be persuaded to survey. It is the first thing on St. Bernard's list, which means the parish either has been quiet or has not been looked at.

Description

A chapel on a shell mound seven miles out in the marsh, reachable at low water and cut off at high. It was a customs station before it was anything, handed on to an order that had already been asked to leave two parishes, and out of contact with anybody who might have corrected it long before the surveys stopped listing the mound at all.

There are no windows above waist height. What light gets in comes off the water and moves on the ceiling, and every image in the building is of somebody going under willingly: the plaster over the door, the painted boards behind the altar, the small figures worked into the rail. The font is full and has never been filled. It has been drained four times and was full again inside the hour, with water that is not the marsh's.

The floor is cut over with names. It has been relaid twice, each time because they ran out of floor, and the old slabs were not carried away: they were turned face down and laid under the new ones. The most recent name is cut in a hand nobody has matched to anybody living.

Procedure

Held by owning the mound and the water around it. No permanent staff. Parties go in at low water, four at a time, and are counted out by name at the boat.

GM Information

There is more mound underneath than on top, and the passages run below the waterline without being wet. People come out of them having been gone longer than they were, and the ones who come out singing are kept apart from the ones who do not. Count the party out by name yourself, out loud, at the boat: do it once when nothing is wrong so the players learn the ritual, and the second time will do all the work for you.

The House on Tupelo Street ACTIVE
L-ORL-840-0001 · Anomaly
Procurement

Acquired by not demolishing it. The buy-out took the whole street and the contractor's schedule has this address crossed out in a different pen. First on the Orleans Parish list, which surprises everybody who reads the file and is only because the parish list is new.

Description

A single-story house on a street where the other eleven lots are slab and weeds. The neighbourhood was bought out after the water came through and everything else was taken down. The paperwork for this one exists, is signed by everybody it needed to be, and was never acted on.

Nothing in the house stays where it is put, and nothing in it will leave. Take a drawer handle, a doorstop, a jar of buttons: the house does not come after you, but the thing you took keeps doing the house's work wherever you set it down. A borrowed spoon turns a kitchen drawer into the sound of somebody going through it. Put it back and the kitchen is a kitchen again within the hour.

The plaster carries faces that surface and go under the way something does in water. None of them is anybody named on the deed.

Procedure

The lot is fenced and the utilities are live, because the house is quieter with the power on. Anything removed is returned by the person who removed it, who is not disciplined for taking it, on the reasoning that discipline makes people hide things.

GM Information

There is no plan to take it down, only drafts, and every one was written by somebody who went inside first to measure up. Let a player pocket something small without comment and do not mention it again for a session. The haunting is portable, it is theirs now, and the moment worth building to is the table working out which of them is carrying it.

The Pumping Station ACTIVE
L-UNK-000-0007 · Anomaly
Procurement

Bought off a water board that was glad to be rid of it, for less than the land was worth, with no survey. Nobody recorded who identified it or how, so it sits at seventh on the unknowns. The purchase file is complete and the acquisition file is a single page.

Description

A Victorian waterworks, decommissioned in the fifties, still drawing power from a supply that was cut in the same decade. Every room is exactly as cold as the room it was in an hour ago, so the building runs about a day behind the weather.

Procedure

Contained by owning it, fencing it, and keeping it on the register as a listed building nobody may enter. Temperature is logged from outside, hourly, by an instrument on a pole.

GM Information

The lag is not stable. Twice it has run three days behind, and on both occasions somebody inside reported hearing the building being used. That is the hook and it is better unexplained: a place that is a day behind is a curiosity, and a place that is three days behind has people in it. If the party go in during a drift, let them hear the pumps working and find the pumps cold.

The Spread ACTIVE
L-UNK-000-0095 · Anomaly
Procurement

Found because it ate a road. A county surveyor could not work out why a resurfacing job had come out eleven meters short of the survey and went to look at the ground. Ninety-fifth on the unknowns.

Description

An irregular patch of ground about forty-five meters by thirty.

It converts what it touches. Soil, asphalt, brick, steel, standing water, anything at the edge becomes more of it, and what it becomes is not chemically or structurally related to what it was.

The rate is constant and it has been measured to four decimal places: a little under ninety centimeters an hour, in every direction, in all weathers.

Unrestrained it would leave any site inside four hours, cover four square kilometers in a day, and reach a thousand kilometers in under a month. Those figures are in the file, in that order, and they are the whole of the case for the arrangement described below.

Noise stops it.

Anything above about sixty decibels halts growth where the sound falls. Machinery works. Speakers work. So do voices, instruments, a dog, a television, and a room of people having an argument, and all of them work equally, and nobody has found a difference between a loudspeaker and a conversation.

Procedure

There is a site on top of it.

That was the solution and it was arrived at after eleven years of loudspeakers, generators, standby generators and one power failure. A building with people in it is never quiet. A building with people in it has never once fallen below the threshold in forty years.

So the site is staffed above its operational need, deliberately, and the staff do not know why the canteen is open at four in the morning or why the gym is where it is.

The quiet hours are covered by rotation. There is a night shift whose duties are light and whose attendance is not.

A fire alarm test is conducted weekly and is not a test.

GM Information

Soak - · Attack - · Defense - · Health Levels - · Movement Rate 0.87m per hour

Traits: It Converts Everything - at eighty-seven centimeters an hour, in all directions, in any weather. Noise Holds It - anything over sixty decibels, and a conversation works as well as a speaker. Four Hours - is how long the site has if the building goes quiet.

The party work there for a year before anybody tells them. Let them complain about the noise.

The session is a power cut at three in the morning, and the answer is not equipment. The answer is that somebody has to go and wake everybody up and keep them talking until it is fixed.

Never let them see the edge move. They should hear the room go quiet and know what that means.

The Bar STATIC
L-GEN-000-0049
Procurement

Located by pattern rather than by report: a square kilometer of wetland that turns up in satellite imagery in places that had no wetland the month before. Forty-ninth on the list of shapes.

Description

A square kilometer of forested wetland with a round pond in the middle of it, walled all the way round by mangroves that push back anything trying to get between them.

It moves. It has been in the southeastern United States, in west Africa, and in south Asia, and it has never turned up anywhere that is not warm and wet. Nobody knows how far it can go or what decides.

There is one way in, on the south side, and it leads to a pontoon walkway across the pond.

At the end of the walkway there is a barn. Two hundred meters square, ten high, on a pier. It has no doors. The way in is a corrugated iron shack stuck onto the south wall.

Outside the shack there is a man on a lawn chair.

He looks like whatever the local people would expect a friendly, slightly odd bartender to look like, and he speaks whatever they speak, and this changes when the wetland changes continents. Nobody has seen him change. He is simply correct for wherever he is.

He is hospitable. He is funny. He is very pleased to see anybody who has got that far, and he has never asked anybody for anything.

The inside of the barn is not described in this entry.

Procedure

It is not contained and cannot be.

It is tracked. Imagery, local reporting and one satellite pass a week, and a standing arrangement with two national mapping agencies who think they are helping with something else.

When it arrives somewhere, a team goes, and the team does not go in. They sit outside the mangroves, count who goes down the walkway and count who comes back, and the two numbers have always matched.

Personnel are not to accept a drink. The instruction is in the procedure and is not explained.

GM Information

Soak - · Attack - · Defense - · Health Levels - · Movement Rate -

Traits: It Moves - continents, without warning, always somewhere warm and wet. One Way In - the south gap, the walkway, the shack. Exactly The Host You Expected - in your language, with your kind of manner. Everybody Comes Back - so far, and the counts are in the file.

Play him as the best company in the register. He should be a relief.

Never describe the inside of the barn. Let a player go in and then cut to them coming out.

They will ask why not the drink. Nobody in the file knows either.

The Roadside VOLATILE
P-GEN-000-0061
Procurement

Found by a planning officer who could not work out which company had built a diner on a supermarket parking lot overnight, and who could not find it in any application, and who eventually went and asked them. Sixty-first on the list of shapes.

Description

A flowering plant whose flower is a roadside diner.

The root system goes down into the water mains and the sewers under whatever paved ground it has taken, aggressively, and that is usually the first sign anybody has that it is there.

The flower is a building. It has the right shape, the right fittings, the right counter, the right signs, and it works. Everything in it is made of plant cells: mostly the sort found in leaves and petals, arranged into laminate, vinyl, steel and glass well enough to pass anything short of a microscope.

Looked at closely from outside, the details are slightly wrong, and the list of what is wrong is in an appendix and is worth reading.

It produces fruit that looks and tastes like the food on a diner menu, including things that should have meat in them and things that should be liquid. All of it is plant tissue. Each piece has a number of small black seeds in it.

The seeds only germinate after they have been through a person.

Eat, pass the seeds, and if that happens on a large enough piece of paved ground there is a new one there within six hours.

The fruit does not compel anybody to do anything. It does cause urgency, at a rate well above the control, and the file is dry about this.

The flower stays open for the life of the plant, except where local licensing says a place like that cannot open at certain hours, at which point it closes into a large bud until the hours are over.

It gives off something that smells of bacon fat.

Anybody breathing it finds that unusual things happening in or near the building are unremarkable and not worth looking at. It is a light touch and it is very effective, and it is why nobody has ever reported one.

Procedure

Instances are identified through water authority reports of root damage, which is the only channel that has ever worked.

Removal is by root excavation followed by chemical treatment, and the paved area is relaid.

Staff working a site wear respirators, which is unpopular, and the reason is explained to them every time.

Nobody eats anything. The instruction is worded to include the phrase just to see.

GM Information

Soak - · Attack - · Defense - · Health Levels - · Movement Rate -

Traits: A Flower - about fifteen meters across, with parking. Seeded Through People - eat, and the ground does the rest. Six Hours - from seed to open. Nothing Odd Here - the smell sees to that, and it is why nobody calls it in.

Play it as a diner. The coffee is good and the staff are the plant.

Let the group eat before anybody has read the file.

The licensing detail is the joke and it is also the tell. Have one close at eleven.

One the city closed, and one the Foundation put inside a working one.

The Ward ACTIVE
L-UNK-000-0014 · Anomaly
Procurement

Bought three years after the hospital went bankrupt, on the strength of a local story about it being haunted, which is normally the sort of report that goes in a drawer. This one was checked because the same drawer already had four accounts of the same noise from four people who had no reason to know each other. Fourteenth on the unknowns.

Description

A closed hospital, kept in good repair by people who have never explained to the council why. Power on, roof sound, grass cut.

Some people who go inside hear crying. Not all of them, and there is no pattern anybody has found in who does: it is not hearing, because two of them were deaf, and it is not suggestion, because the first four did not know what they were walking into. Those who hear it describe it identically, which is the part of the file that ends arguments.

It comes from the old maternity floor. It is faint at the door and no louder anywhere, and the distance never closes no matter how far in you go.

People who hear it want to keep going. They do not describe this as fear. They describe it as being needed, and they are annoyed at being stopped.

The hospital's own records list every birth that did not survive it. The count in that ledger and the number of separate voices people report are the same number, and nobody has published that.

Procedure

Fenced, locked, and two on the gate turning away anybody who comes to see the haunted hospital, which is a surprising number of people in October. Three more patrol the upper floors. Anybody who reports the noise is escorted out, whatever their seniority, and is not allowed back on the strength of feeling fine about it.

GM Information

Soak - · Attack - · Defense - · Health Levels - · Movement Rate -

Nothing here can be fought, and nothing here has ever hurt anybody directly.

Traits: Some People Hear It - pick before play which characters can, and do not roll for it; it is not a perception check and there is no pattern to find. Never Closer - the sound is the same volume at the door and at the end of the corridor. Wanted - those who hear it read the pull as being needed rather than as being drawn, and will argue, reasonably and at length, with anybody who tries to take them out.

Tell one or two players privately that their character hears crying, and tell the rest nothing. Everything good about this location happens between the players: the ones who hear it have a reason to go up, the ones who do not think their friends are behaving strangely, and both are right. Do not put anything at the top of the stairs. The ledger is the payoff, and it should be found after they leave.

The Annex HOLDING
L-GEN-000-0012 · Site. Base 2
Procurement

The arrangement, rather than the building. Four hospitals carry one of these and the paperwork is identical in all four, down to the wording of the lease. Twelfth on the shapes list. Which four is a separate document and a higher clearance.

Description

A wing of a working hospital, entered through a door marked for medical records, staffed by people whose pay comes from somewhere the hospital has never audited. Four beds, two cells, an incinerator with its own chimney.

Useful because ambulances arrive there all night and nobody counts them.

Procedure

Staff are hospital staff on paper and are rotated through real clinical work to keep it that way. Nothing is admitted during daylight. The incinerator is run on a schedule that matches the hospital's own.

GM Information

It is inside a hospital. Everything that gets out is immediately among the most fragile people in the county, and the players will know that before you say it. Use this site when you want a containment problem the party cannot solve with force, because every loud answer is being given twenty feet from a ward. The four beds are for their own people, which is worth establishing before one of them needs it.

The Morgue ACTIVE
L-ORL-840-0008 · Anomaly
Procurement

Reported by a mortuary technician who had called the orderlies four times about a drawer that would not stay shut and who, the fifth time, looked in it. Eighth on the Orleans Parish list.

Description

A hospital morgue in a basement. It was an ordinary morgue for most of a century and then it was not, and nothing about the building changed.

Drawers open on their own.

One at a time, unpredictably, a few times a month. Inside there is a body under a sheet, and the body is not one of the hospital's and is not on any list.

It is somebody who is currently alive.

They are always a patient in that hospital or a member of its staff, and they are always somebody who will die there, and the drawer opens between four hours and nine days before they do.

The body in the drawer is accurate to the moment of death: the injuries, the color, the hospital gown, the cannula in the same arm. Two of them have been photographed and compared afterwards and the photographs match.

Interference has been attempted eleven times.

Seven of those people died anyway, of the thing in the drawer, on schedule. Three died of something else, sooner, and one of those was caused by the intervention. One lived, and the file notes that he was discharged, and notes what happened to him nine months later.

The body vanishes when the person dies. There is never anything to bury twice.

Procedure

The basement is Foundation and the hospital thinks it is a plant room. The morgue above ground is new and is where the hospital's dead go.

The drawers are watched. When one opens, the person is identified from the face, which is usually quick, and the identification goes to one officer.

He does nothing with it. That is the procedure. It is reviewed annually, it has been upheld every year since the eleven, and the reasoning is the eleven.

The log of names is sealed and is kept, and the officer writes the date of death beside each one.

GM Information

Soak - · Attack - · Defense - · Health Levels - · Movement Rate -

Traits: Four Hours To Nine Days - and it is always somebody who will die in that building. Accurate To The Moment - injuries, color, the cannula in the same arm. Eleven Attempts - seven unchanged, three worse, one discharged.

Open the drawer on somebody the party know. Let them look at the face.

The rule is do nothing. Make them argue about it at the table and make the counter-argument good: eleven attempts, and the file is on the wall.

Whatever they choose, do not tell them whether they were right. The officer with the log has been not knowing for nineteen years.

The Mausoleum DORMANT
L-ORL-840-0006 · Anomaly
Procurement

Reported by a survey team recording damaged tombs for a drainage project, who photographed the inscription because of the last line and sent it on because of the last line. Sixth on the Orleans Parish list.

Description

A granite mausoleum, badly damaged, in a cemetery that has not taken a burial in a long time.

The inscription on one side gives a name, a date of birth in the eighteen forties, a date of death in the nineteen tens, and an age. Under that, cut in the same hand, at the same time, in letters the same size:

HE WAS RIGHT

The name is not a name anybody can place. The parish burial register for that year exists, in a church archive across the river, and does not contain it.

The grave was opened once. There is a body, of the right age, dead for about the right length of time, killed by gunfire at close range. Several shots.

That is all the mausoleum does now, and it is why the class reads DORMANT rather than STATIC: it did other things, for about eleven months after it was found, and then it stopped, and the eleven months are in the file.

The file gives them plainly and does not summarize them. Nobody has proposed an explanation and the last note on the matter is nine words long.

Procedure

Fenced. The cemetery is bought and the drainage project was routed around it at a cost that is on the record as an environmental constraint.

Nothing is excavated. Nothing is repaired: the damage is original and is documented and the instruction is that it stays as it is.

The inscription is photographed annually and the photographs are compared. It has not changed.

GM Information

Soak - · Attack - · Defense - · Health Levels - · Movement Rate -

Traits: Three Words - cut at the same time as the dates, by the same hand. Not In The Register - the name is in no burial record anywhere. Eleven Months - of something, and then nothing, for decades.

The whole entry is a photograph. Show them the inscription and let them read the last line, and do not explain it, ever.

The eleven months should be a folder the party find and read in the car. Write four incidents, make them small and specific, and stop.

If a player asks what he was right about, that is the campaign, and the answer is that the file does not know either.

The Memorial Park ACTIVE
L-FL-840-0004 · Anomaly
Procurement

Came in when a contractor laying a drain under the north- east corner of a disused pet cemetery found a human femur and did the right thing. Fourth on the Florida list.

Description

A pet memorial park of about four acres, closed for decades, with a few hundred small headstones under live oaks.

Most of it is what it says it is. Dogs, cats, birds, and one horse, buried properly, with stones cut by a local mason whose order books survive.

Under the north-east corner there are people.

Nine so far. They are buried at the ordinary depth, without coffins, in the clothes they died in, under headstones that give a pet's name and a pet's dates and a short line of the kind people put on a pet's grave.

Four of them are the same person.

Genetically identical, buried under the same name, on four different dates across nineteen years, and the pathology puts the age at death within a couple of years each time. The mason's order book has all four entries, placed by hand, and the handwriting in the book is the same each time and is not the mason's.

The remaining five are five different people and none of them matches a missing person.

Nothing happens at the site. There are no manifestations, no effects, and nothing has been observed in eleven years of monitoring. New stones do not appear.

What appears is the order book.

It is in the county archive, it is a real Victorian ledger that has been there since the practice closed, and about once a year there is a new entry in it in that same hand, dated for a burial that has not happened.

Procedure

The park is owned and fenced and maintained as a nature reserve.

Nothing is exhumed. The nine were recovered before the current instruction and are held, and the instruction now says they are to be reinterred where they were, and that has not been done, and the reason is in the file and is a disagreement between two departments.

The order book is checked monthly. It is not removed from the archive, because the archive is where the entries appear, and this was tested once.

GM Information

Soak - · Attack - · Defense - · Health Levels - · Movement Rate -

Traits: Nine People Under The Corner - with pets' names on the stones. Four Of Them Are One Man - nineteen years apart, the same age each time. The Book Is Still Being Written In - once a year, for a burial that has not happened.

Run the archive, not the cemetery. A reading room, a ledger, and a party comparing handwriting.

The new entry is the session. A date, a name, a line about a good dog, and the date is eleven weeks away.

Do not tell them whether anything will be there on the day. Take them to the corner and let them dig.

The Lost STATIC
L-MO-840-0003
Procurement

A town in Missouri where the undertaker's paperwork did not add up, and had not added up for a long time, and nobody had ever queried it. Third in Missouri.

Description

About one in five people who die in that town get up again.

It happens between eleven at night and three in the morning, usually within a few days of death. There is no pattern in who: age, health, manner of death, none of it predicts anything.

They walk to the nearest concentration of trash. Mostly that means the town dump, which is at the end of the county road, and lately it has meant smaller things: a dumpster, a trash enclosure, the bottom of somebody's garden.

When they get there they get into it and cover themselves over.

Then they stop, and they are a body again, and they can be taken back and buried and nothing further happens.

Ninety-five of them are on record.

The town calls them lost souls.

The town is not frightened of this. It has been happening long enough to be part of how the place works. People see one on the road at night and do not stop and do not talk about it the next day, and when asked why, the answers given are about respect, and are given in a tone that does not invite a second question.

Some of the interviews suggest that people are not always able to tell the difference between one of them and somebody out walking.

The file is careful about that suggestion and does not extend it.

Procedure

The town is monitored through the surgery, the funeral director and the county dump, all of which are on the payroll.

Nothing is interfered with. Bodies are recovered from the waste after they have settled and are returned to the families, who receive them without comment.

There was a plan to move the town. It is in the file, struck through, with the date and the name of the person who struck it through.

The dump is not closed. There was a proposal for that too.

GM Information

Soak 2 · Attack 1 · Defense 2 · Health Levels 4 · Movement Rate 4

They have never attacked anybody. They are going somewhere.

Traits: One In Five - and nothing predicts which. Between Eleven And Three - and they walk, and they do not hurry. To The Trash - the dump, a dumpster, whatever is nearest. The Town Knows - and the town lets them go.

Play the town first. It is friendly and it is normal and it is not hiding anything.

Somebody walks past the car at night. Do not say which they were.

The struck-through plan is the entry. Somebody decided to leave these people alone.

The Gallows TERMINAL
L-LA-840-0004
Procurement

A mass grave outside New Orleans was found in nineteen ten and the structures beside it were still standing and are still standing. Fourth in Louisiana.

Description

A set of gallows put up in the eighteen fifties, close together, next to ground where a great many people were buried without names.

The timber has not rotted. It has been out in that climate for a hundred and seventy years and it is sound.

Hang somebody from one and they do not die.

They go on breathing. The lungs work and the air goes in and out and the blood carries it, and every measurement anybody can take says the person is fine.

They experience strangulation. All of it, continuously, at full strength.

Four to six minutes in, they stop responding and appear to be dead. Pulse, pupils, everything: a competent doctor will certify it.

They are not dead. What is happening inside is in the medical annex and the annex is short because everything in it is one sentence repeated with different measurements.

Cutting them down ends it. They come round in a few minutes and they remember every second of it and they are able to say how long it felt.

Nobody has been left on one. The prohibition is absolute and predates our involvement.

There are eleven of them and all eleven work.

Procedure

The site is fenced and is held under a historical preservation designation that is genuine and is administered by us.

Nothing is hung from them. Not a person, not an animal, not a weight.

The timber is not removed, sampled, treated or repaired.

The ground is not disturbed. The mass grave is mapped and is left, and the names that have been recovered are on a list that is kept with the file.

The site is visited annually and the visit is a condition check.

GM Information

Soak - · Attack 8 · Defense - · Health Levels - · Movement Rate -

Traits: A Hundred And Seventy Years, No Rot - in that climate, outdoors. You Do Not Die - the breathing continues and the blood carries air. You Feel All Of It - continuously, and you can say how long it felt. Cut Them Down And They Come Back - remembering every second.

Nobody is hanged in the session. The horror is that the ropes are still there.

Somebody will ask what happens if you leave a person on one. Nobody knows.

Read out the list of recovered names. That is the end of the scene.

The School ACTIVE
L-UNK-000-0141 · Anomaly
Procurement

Condemned decades ago after a surveyor found that the floor plan did not agree with the drawings, and acquired properly after four local disappearances were all traced back to an afternoon in the building. One hundred and forty-first on the unknowns.

Description

A three-story school with two wings and a foyer between them, boarded, on a site nobody has redeveloped.

Inside it is bigger. Corridors run further than the wing does, there are more classrooms than there are windows, and the staircases arrive on floors that the outside does not have.

The extra space is school. Not a maze and not a distortion: proper rooms, properly fitted out, with desks and boards and coat hooks at the right height, in the same period as the rest of the building.

They are in use.

Not by anybody visible. The boards have work on them, in chalk, in a teacher's hand, and the work changes. Desks have bags under them. There are coats on the hooks in October that are not there in June.

The bell rings. It is on a schedule that has been recorded and is consistent, and it is not the schedule the school used when it was open.

Nothing has ever been seen. Cameras left running produce eight hours of empty rooms and a bell.

People go missing in it. Not many and not often: four before acquisition and two since, all of them alone, all of them in the extra space rather than the real building.

Nobody has come back and nothing has been found.

Procedure

Sealed, fenced, alarmed, and patrolled by a firm that believes it is preventing vandalism.

Entry is in threes, roped, with the rule that nobody passes out of sight of the person behind them. The rope is not symbolic and has been used.

The boards are photographed on every visit. The work on them is at about the level of a nine-year-old and it is marked.

GM Information

Soak - · Attack - · Defense - · Health Levels - · Movement Rate -

Traits: More School Than Building - proper rooms, correctly fitted, in the right period. Somebody Is Teaching - the boards change, the coats are seasonal, the bell has its own schedule. Six Gone - all alone, all in the extra space.

Run the visit by the rules: three people, a rope, nobody out of sight. Make the players enforce it on each other.

The marking is the detail. Photograph a board one month and the same board the next, and have the work corrected in red.

Nobody appears, ever. If the party break the rule, the character is simply not behind them any more, and the rope is on the floor.

The Building ACTIVE
